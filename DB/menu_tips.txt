Bioavailability: Nutrients in food aren't always 100% absorbed. For example, plant-based iron (non-heme) is absorbed less efficiently than animal-based iron (heme) unless paired with Vitamin C. A meal plan must account for how foods interact.
Separate calcium intake (dairy) from iron intake, as calcium blocks iron absorption.

Glycemic Load (GL) Management: To prevent insulin spikes that encourage fat storage, pair your 279g of carbohydrates with dietary fiber (target 34g) and protein. Prioritize complex carbs (oats, sweet potatoes, legumes) over simple sugars.

tailored to your age, sex, activity level, genetic predispositions, and medical history (e.g., insulin resistance, allergies).

Fat-Soluble Vitamins: Consume Vitamins A, D, E, and K with your fat sources (e.g., olive oil, avocados, or eggs) to ensure they are absorbed through the intestinal wall.



Phytonutrients/Zoochemicals: Not assigned a raw milligram value, but achieved by eating across the color spectrum (anthocyanins in berries, lycopene in tomatoes) and consuming quality animal products (creatine and carnosine in meat).


Black tea consumed at breakfast contains tannins that significantly inhibit the absorption of iron from oats. To optimize the absorption of plant-based iron, it is recommended to drink tea between meals.

Diversity of protein sources: no single food family (soy, wheat gluten, ...) should dominate the day. At most 2 soy foods a day, and soy should bring less than about half of the protein of the day. Spread the protein over other sources: lentils, chickpeas, beans, peas, seitan, nuts and seeds. Lecithin taken in a few grams does not count as a soy food.
